# 03 — Creator Market Entry: Reimagined in Light

## Strategic goal

Before launching an expensive artist-collaboration program, prove that people care about the core experience:

> **What happens when a familiar piece of music is reinterpreted as a visual performance through light?**

The content is not the end product.

It is a low-cost mechanism for:

- audience acquisition
- user research
- product dogfooding
- creator education
- market discovery
- partner recruitment

---

## 1. Why start with content

A conventional software launch often looks like:

**feature → tutorial → promotion**

This strategy starts with the experience:

**expressive work  
→ audience curiosity  
→ explanation of how it was made  
→ creator interest  
→ product trial  
→ feedback  
→ product improvement**

The audience should first think:

> “I want to make something like this.”

Only then:

> “What tool made it possible?”

---

## 2. The first content format

Working names:

- **Reimagined in Light**
- **Song to Light**
- **What Would This Song Look Like?**

### Episode structure

1. **Cold open** — 5–10 seconds of the final visual result
2. **Creative brief** — what does the song feel like and what do we want to reinterpret?
3. **Visual translation** — colour, space, movement, intensity, density, timing, restraint
4. **Build in EMU** — show the workflow
5. **Performance layer** — dancer / creator / small performance setup
6. **Final cut** — polished 30–90 second audiovisual piece
7. **Product reflection** — what worked, what was awkward, what should be tested next?

---

## 3. Why older / catalogue music can be useful

The purpose is not nostalgia alone.

Older catalogue music may already carry:

- cultural recognition
- fan memory
- strong visual identity
- established choreography / performance associations
- lower competition with current-release promotion

That creates an audience bridge:

**nostalgia / fandom  
→ performance curiosity  
→ visual-expression interest  
→ EMU discovery**

Rights remain a real business constraint. “Old” does not automatically mean easy or cheap to license.

A content slate should score:

- creative fit
- audience potential
- licensing feasibility
- cost
- speed
- partner value

---

## 4. Example: an older K-pop performance track

JYJ’s *Get Out* is a useful conceptual example because its original MV has strong visual memory:

- red-dominant visual language
- indoor / outdoor contrast
- performance staging
- choreography and pose
- dramatic spatial changes

The goal would not be a shot-for-shot remake.

The design question is:

> **If we reduce the original visual identity to its emotional and spatial principles, how would we reinterpret it today with light as the primary medium?**

That makes the exercise a reinterpretation rather than a reproduction.

---

## 5. Why dance creators are a strong beachhead market

Dance creators already work at the intersection of:

**music + movement + camera + visual identity**

Many want their content to feel more complete than a standard cover video.

Their job-to-be-done may be:

> **“Help me turn choreography into a more distinctive visual interpretation of the music.”**

That makes several adjacent segments worth testing:

- dance studios
- dance crews
- choreographers
- dance teachers
- K-pop cover creators
- performance-content creators

---

## 6. Product implications from dance

Dance may expose needs that musician-only research misses.

### Performance structure
Lighting may need to respond to:
- formation change
- centre position
- choreography hit
- dance break
- pose / freeze
- entrance / exit
- first chorus vs final chorus

This suggests a future research layer:

**music structure + performance structure**

### Repeated-section variation
Repeated choruses may use different choreography and should not always receive identical visual treatment.

### Creator-language templates
Research concepts could include:
- centre focus
- silhouette hit
- chorus expansion
- restrained verse
- final-pose freeze
- wide spatial reveal
- controlled strobe accent

These are discovery concepts, not committed features.

### Small-space workflow
Studios may value:
- simple fixture layouts
- fast calibration
- repeatable camera-friendly setups
- reusable creator templates
- minimal need for a lighting technician

## 7. The market funnel

### Layer 1 — Spectacle
TikTok / Reels / Shorts:
> “I want my videos to look like this.”

### Layer 2 — Curiosity
YouTube full episode:
> “How did they make this?”

### Layer 3 — Learning
Tutorial / breakdown:
> “I could try this.”

### Layer 4 — Creation
Starter project / template / setup guidance:
> “I made my own version.”

### Layer 5 — Sharing
Creator remixes / community submissions:
> “I want other people to see what I made.”

---

## 8. Bootstrap progression

### Stage 1 — Product-team reinterpretation
Use original, commissioned or clearly licensed music.

Goal:
- prove the format
- dogfood EMU
- learn which visual problems are interesting

### Stage 2 — Dance-creator collaboration
Add crews, choreographers and studios.

Goal:
- test the adjacent market
- increase performance value
- increase shareability

### Stage 3 — Independent artist collaboration
Move from interpreting **an artist’s work** to interpreting **with the artist**.

### Stage 4 — EMU Artist Lab
Artists become design partners and recurring product-discovery participants.

### Stage 5 — Larger artists / catalogue partnerships
Use recognisable names as amplifiers after the format proves itself.

---

## 9. What would validate the market

The hypothesis strengthens if:

- creators ask to use the setup again
- studios see commercial value in light-enabled sessions
- viewers ask how to reproduce the workflow
- EMU downloads follow content
- creators make independent reinterpretations
- lighting becomes part of their recurring visual identity

It weakens if:

- engagement comes only from fandom / nostalgia
- creators enjoy the shoot but do not want to use EMU
- workflow requires heavy expert support
- studios see no repeat business value
- no product-learning signal emerges

The content succeeds only if it helps answer a market question.
