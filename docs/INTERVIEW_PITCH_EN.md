# ENTTEC Product Specialist Interview Pitch (English)

> Goal: do **not** try to present a full strategy in the first interview.
>
> Use 5–10 minutes to demonstrate three things:
>
> 1. I understand the product and its users.
> 2. I can move from user problems to product hypotheses, not just feature ideas.
> 3. I know when to ask, when to test an assumption, and when to stop and listen.

---

## 0. Principle: ask first, pitch second

This is **not** a speech script.

The best sequence is:

1. Let Ebony explain why the role changed from “EMU Product Specialist” to “Product Specialist, not limited to EMU.”
2. Choose only the 1–2 modules that fit her answer.
3. Pause after each idea and let her respond.
4. Never present your hypothesis as ENTTEC’s existing roadmap.

The mindset:

> **I am not here to tell ENTTEC what it should do. I am here to show how I would understand users, form hypotheses, test them, and contribute to product decisions.**

---

# 1. 30–45 second opening: why this problem interests me

My interest did not start from lighting software itself.

It started from a broader question:

**How does an artist want light to participate in the expression of music?**

One experience that influenced me was Ryuichi Sakamoto’s *seeing sound, hearing time* exhibition in Beijing. It made me realise that sound, light, image, space and timing do not have to be linked through simple synchronisation. They can work together to express the creator’s intent.

Later, experiences such as Hue + Spotify made the gap clearer to me:

> “lights reacting to music” is not the same as “light participating in artistic expression.”

### Suggested wording

> One of the experiences that first made me think seriously about music and light was Ryuichi Sakamoto’s *seeing sound, hearing time* exhibition in Beijing.
>
> What stayed with me was that the visual layer wasn’t simply synchronized to sound. Light, space and timing were part of the artistic expression itself.
>
> That’s why I became interested in a slightly different question: how can we help musicians express what they want visually, instead of only making lights react to audio?

**Pause here.**

Do not jump immediately into AI, Artist Lab or ELM.

---

# 2. Ask about the role before sharing the strategy

This is the most important entry point.

> Since the role is now broader than EMU, what product problems or opportunities made the team decide to broaden it?

Then:

> When you say “not limited to EMU”, does that mainly mean supporting a broader range of products, or taking broader product responsibility across software and hardware applications?

You want to understand whether “broader” means:

- more SKU support
- more application areas
- real product ownership
- EMU + ELM + hardware portfolio responsibility
- a cross-functional role across customer / product / market

---

# 3. 60–90 second product observation: EMU + ELM

If her answer suggests the role touches software, portfolio thinking or user discovery, share this.

### Observation

ELM 2026 includes a large set of shader effects that originated in EMU.

That suggests a useful **hypothesis**:

> **EMU could be the faster, creator-facing experimentation platform, while ELM turns selected mature creative capabilities into production-grade tools for professional-scale use.**

You do not know whether ENTTEC already thinks this way internally, so frame it explicitly as a hypothesis.

### Suggested wording

> I noticed something interesting in ELM 2026: a large set of shader effects were ported from EMU.
>
> It made me wonder whether EMU could play a broader role as a creator-facing experimentation platform, where new creative interactions are tested with musicians first, while only the ideas that prove useful and controllable graduate into professional-scale workflows in ELM.
>
> I don’t know whether that matches how ENTTEC thinks internally, so I’d be very interested in how you see the relationship between the two products.

**Pause.**

The important signal is not simply whether she says yes or no.

Listen for:
- whether she starts discussing actual product strategy
- whether she shares roadmap logic
- whether Product Specialists participate in these conversations
- whether the role has influence beyond support

---

# 4. Two-minute module: EMU Artist Lab

Only use this if she shows real interest in the musician / creator side.

## Core idea

Do not start with artist endorsement.

Start with:

> **Design partner first, endorsement second.**

Invite independent and mid-scale musicians to use one of their own songs and explain:

- What should the audience feel?
- Where should light follow the music?
- Where should it deliberately *not* follow the beat?
- Should the second chorus behave differently from the first?
- What kind of visual behaviour would ruin the song?

Then observe:

- where they struggle
- where current EMU workflows are awkward
- which requests are one-off preferences
- which problems repeat across artists

### Suggested wording

> One idea I’d love to test is an EMU Artist Lab.
>
> I wouldn’t treat artist partnerships mainly as endorsements. I’d use them as product discovery.
>
> An artist brings one real song and explains what they want the audience to feel. We help translate that into a visual language, build the best version we can with the current product, and document where the workflow breaks down.
>
> The finished audiovisual piece becomes authentic content, but the more important output is product learning.

---

# 5. The strongest storytelling mechanism: the return episode

This is one of the most distinctive parts of the concept, but keep it concise.

### Scenario

An artist asks for a behaviour EMU cannot currently express.

Do not pretend the product can do it.

Say:

> “We can’t do this properly yet.”

Build the best reduced version.

Later:
- a prototype appears
- another artist tests it
- the capability matures
- the original artist returns
- they use a new song and finally build something closer to what they originally imagined

### Suggested wording

> I also think the limitations themselves can become part of the story.
>
> If an artist asks for something EMU cannot do yet, I wouldn’t hide it. We could build the best reduced version, document the product gap, and if the capability is later developed, invite the artist back.
>
> That turns product development into a real narrative: creator intent, limitation, experimentation, product change, and then the artist finally getting closer to what they originally imagined.

This demonstrates:
- product discovery
- roadmap learning
- long-term creator relationships
- authentic content
- brand credibility

# 6. 60–90 second AI module

Use this **only** if Ebony brings up AI, automation, smarter workflow, or your AI background.

Do not open with:

> “I think you should add AI.”

## Core idea

AI should not become:

> sad song = blue light

It should become a:

> **creative translator**

The artist might say:

> “I want the final chorus to feel like the whole space opens up, but I don’t want it to become more aggressive.”

The system can help translate that into:
- musical section
- spatial behaviour
- intensity
- movement
- transition
- sync / drift / restraint
- editable EMU / ELM parameters

### Suggested wording

> If AI becomes relevant, I’d be more interested in using it as a creative translator than as an automatic taste-maker.
>
> The product shouldn’t decide what the music means. It should help the artist express what they mean.
>
> For example, an artist could say, “I want the final chorus to feel much wider, but not more aggressive,” and the system could help translate that into an editable lighting starting point.

Then ask:

> Has the team discussed anything at that level, above the existing audio-reactive workflow?

---

# 7. Content is not separate from product discovery

If the conversation moves into marketing / community:

## YouTube
Long-form:
- artist intent
- process
- failures
- product limitations
- final audiovisual work

## TikTok / Reels / Shorts
30–90 seconds:
- strongest visual result
- one surprising transformation
- one creative idea

The funnel becomes:

**spectacle → curiosity → learning → creation → sharing**

## Comments

Comments should not directly vote the roadmap.

They should generate **hypotheses**.

Example:

> “Can EMU understand lyrics?”

Do not treat 2,000 likes as product validation.

Ask what the deeper problem is:
- lyrical meaning?
- song structure?
- narrative progression?
- wanting light to follow storytelling?

### One-line version

> I’d also use the content itself as a public discovery loop. YouTube can show the full creative process, short-form platforms can show the final visual result, and the comments can generate product hypotheses — not roadmap votes.

---

# 8. How I would define the Product Specialist role

The important message is not:

> “I have many creative ideas.”

It is:

> **I can turn ambiguous user language into a testable product problem.**

### Suggested wording

> I see the Product Specialist role as a bridge from customer input to product decision participation — understanding the problem, testing the hypothesis, and helping decide whether it deserves product investment and where it belongs in the portfolio.

---

# 9. If asked: “What would you do in your first 3–6 months?”

Do **not** say:

> I would immediately build AI / launch Artist Lab.

Use this sequence.

## Days 0–30 — Learn
- use EMU and ELM end-to-end
- shadow Support / Sales / Engineering
- study real support patterns and cases
- understand decision rights
- map the product / user ecosystem

## Days 31–60 — Discover
- interview musicians / producers
- interview ELM professionals / integrators
- identify the 3 strongest repeated problems
- decide whether Artist Lab deserves a pilot

## Days 61–90 — Experiment
- recruit a small number of design partners
- test one workflow / prototype
- define success and kill criteria
- feed results back into product decisions

## Months 4–6 — Prove
- test repeatability
- ship / iterate / kill
- only then assess whether a concept has EMU → ELM portfolio value

### Suggested wording

> I wouldn’t join and immediately try to build my idea.
>
> My first month would be about learning the actual products, users, support patterns and decision process.
>
> Then I’d validate whether the problem I’m imagining is actually repeated across users. Only after that would I propose a small experiment.

This line is important because it shows product discipline rather than founder-style overconfidence.

# 10. Branch based on Ebony’s answer

## A. If the role is mostly Support / Application

Do not force portfolio strategy.

Ask:

> How much of the role is reactive support versus proactive product discovery?

Focus on:
- support → product insight
- user pain synthesis
- small experiments
- feedback loop

---

## B. If the role spans EMU / ELM / hardware

Focus on:
- portfolio relationship
- user segmentation
- creator vs professional workflow
- feature graduation
- shared creative primitives

---

## C. If she brings up AI

Focus on:
- creative translator
- human-in-the-loop
- artist intent
- MVP first
- no “one-click magic” positioning

---

## D. If she is excited by Artist Lab

Focus on:
- independent / mid-scale musicians first
- 3–6 month pilot
- design partner first
- YouTube + short-form
- return episodes
- comments → hypotheses
- metrics beyond views

---

## E. If she says the immediate need is sales support

Do not pretend that automatically fits your long-term goal.

Ask:

> Would the role have any protected responsibility for product feedback, experimentation or roadmap input?

You need to know whether the job is really:

**Product Specialist**

or:

**product-flavoured support.**

---

# 11. What NOT to lead with in a first interview

Unless she explicitly wants more detail, do not start with:

- “I want to invite Sia / Muni Long / LANY”
- detailed artist contract structures
- program budget
- detailed AI architecture
- detailed shared-engine architecture
- “EMU should be ENTTEC’s most experimental product” stated as fact
- “your CEO probably already wants to do this”
- “ENTTEC should change its strategy this way”

Those belong in a later product discussion, case exercise or working session.

# 13. Closing statement

> What excites me about this role is not just learning a lighting product. It’s the possibility of sitting close enough to users, engineering and the product decisions to help turn ambiguous creative needs into something real.
>
> That’s the kind of product work I’d like to grow into.

---

# 14. What success in the interview actually means

Do not evaluate the interview only by:

> “Did Ebony like my ideas?”

You also need evidence on:

- Does the role have real ownership?
- Does Product Specialist have direct user access?
- Does the role participate in product decisions?
- Is Engineering capacity available?
- Can the role run experiments?
- Does “broader scope” mean broader ownership or simply more support?
- Could the role own a domain after 12 months?
- Can a Product Specialist stay involved from user insight to product outcome?

If those answers are weak, then even strong enthusiasm for your ideas may not make this the Product entry role you actually want.
