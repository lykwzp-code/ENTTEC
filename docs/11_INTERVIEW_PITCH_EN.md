# 11 — ENTTEC Interview Story (English)

## The interview is not about presenting a pile of ideas

The story is:

> **why music + light matters to me  
> → the creator problem I see  
> → how EMU could lower the entry barrier without losing depth  
> → why that could expand ENTTEC’s market  
> → how users could grow from EMU into hardware / ELM  
> → how content and Artist Lab validate the idea  
> → when AI becomes relevant  
> → what the Product Specialist role should own**

---

# 1. Start with the origin

> One of the experiences that first made me think seriously about music and light was Ryuichi Sakamoto’s seeing sound, hearing time exhibition in Beijing.
>
> What stayed with me was that light, space and timing were not simply synchronized to sound — they were part of the artistic expression.
>
> Later, experiences such as Hue + Spotify made the gap clearer to me: making lights react to music is not the same as helping a creator express what they want visually.
>
> That led me to a question: how can a creator who is not a professional lighting designer turn an abstract feeling into an executable visual language?

Pause.

---

# 2. Ask why the role changed before pitching

> Since the role is now broader than EMU, what product problems or opportunities made the team decide to broaden it?

Then:

> When you say “not limited to EMU”, does that mainly mean supporting more products, or taking broader product responsibility?

Listen first.

---

# 3. Product model: easy to start, deep enough to grow

If the conversation moves into EMU’s future:

> I’m starting to think the opportunity is not to make EMU simpler by removing professional capability. It may be to make the first successful result dramatically easier, while keeping a path into deeper control.
>
> The product philosophy reminds me a little of what creator tools such as CapCut did for video editing: you do not need to become a professional editor before you can create, but the tool can reveal more control as your ambition grows.

Then explain:

### Quick / Template
> I just want a good music-light result quickly.

### Creator
> I want this to feel more like me.

### Advanced
> I want precise lighting control.

Key line:

> **I’d think about EMU as easy to start, but deep enough to grow with the creator.**

---

# 4. Why this can expand the category

> If EMU can do that, it no longer serves only people who already know they need lighting software.
>
> A dance creator or small production team may start from “I want this video to feel more expressive,” not “I need a DMX platform.”
>
> That creates the possibility of expanding the category rather than only competing for existing professional lighting users.

Key line:

> **EMU could create new lighting users, not only compete for existing ones.**

---

# 5. User ladder across ENTTEC

A possible progression:

**Creator → Prosumer → Professional**

Start:
- EMU
- templates
- simple setup

Grow:
- deeper customization
- more fixtures
- ENTTEC hardware

Later:
- larger / spatial work
- ELM
- deployment products where relevant

Portfolio sentence:

> **EMU creates new creators.  
> ENTTEC hardware equips them as their physical setups grow.  
> ELM scales mature creative work into professional production.**

The objective is not forced lock-in.

It is to make ENTTEC the easiest ecosystem to grow with.

---

# 6. Validate the market cheaply first

> I would not start by spending heavily on major artists or building a large AI feature.
>
> I’d first test whether people actually care about reinterpreting music visually through light, and whether that interest translates into creator demand.

Content format:

**Reimagined in Light / Song to Light**

Process:
1. rights-cleared music
2. define visual language
3. reinterpret in EMU
4. add dancer / creator where useful
5. YouTube full process
6. TikTok / Shorts final performance
7. observe who actually learns, downloads, builds and returns

---

# 7. Why dance / older K-pop can be useful

> An older K-pop track such as JYJ’s Get Out is interesting as a conceptual example because it already carries strong visual memory — red, choreography, performance and dramatic space.
>
> The point would not be to recreate the original MV, but to reinterpret its emotional and spatial identity through light.

Potential adjacent market:

- dance studios
- dance crews
- choreographers
- K-pop cover creators
- performance-content creators

Job-to-be-done:

> **Help me make my performance a visual interpretation of the music, not just a recording of choreography.**

---

# 8. Music-to-Light Visual Grammar

What I want to learn with musicians is not simply:

> drums / bass / vocals / synths → lights

It is:

> **What do those elements mean in the artist’s own creative language?**

Research model:

**musical element  
→ artist-defined role  
→ artistic intent  
→ desired visual relationship  
→ visual primitive  
→ EMU implementation**

Ask:
- Which elements are the main characters?
- Which create tension, release, intimacy, movement or atmosphere?
- Which should sync directly?
- Which should delay, drift or counterpoint?
- Which should remain visually quiet?
- What visual treatment would feel completely wrong?

Learn the grammar manually before automating it.

---

# 9. Artist Lab

If the market signal is real, evolve into Artist Lab.

Principle:

> **Design partner first, endorsement second.**

Artists become part of product discovery.

A limitation can become a return episode:
- first version cannot fully express the idea
- prototype later
- artist returns to validate the improved capability

---

# 10. Community and brand

Comments are hypothesis sources, not roadmap votes.

The creator-facing brand promise could become:

> **We help creators learn to express with light.**

This creates a loop across:
- product
- content
- education
- community
- creator partnerships

---

# 11. AI comes later

Only expand if the interviewer brings up AI / automation.

> **AI should be a creative translator, not an automatic taste-maker.**

The creator remains the authority.

---

# 12. Bring it back to Product Specialist

The message is not:

> “I have many strategic ideas.”

It is:

> **I want to turn ambiguous user language into testable product problems, then stay close enough to experimentation, decision-making, launch and learning to build real product judgement.**

Suggested line:

> I see the Product Specialist role as a bridge from customer input to product decision participation.

---

# 13. First 3–6 months

### Days 0–30 — Learn
- use EMU as beginner and advanced user
- understand ELM / hardware
- shadow Product / Engineering / Support / Sales
- map real user friction

### Days 31–60 — Discover
- interview musicians, dance creators, existing users and non-users
- identify top repeated problems
- test beginner-to-advanced progression hypothesis

### Days 61–90 — Experiment
- one progressive-complexity prototype
- 2–3 Reimagined in Light pilots
- first Music-to-Light workshop

### Months 4–6 — Prove
- qualified acquisition
- retention / customization
- hardware attachment
- then decide Artist Lab / AI / EMU→ELM

---

# 14. Two-minute compact version

> My interest in this space started from Ryuichi Sakamoto’s seeing sound, hearing time exhibition in Beijing. It made me realise that light shouldn’t just react to music — it can participate in artistic expression.
>
> That’s why EMU interests me. I think it could potentially become much more accessible without losing its professional depth: quick templates for people who just want a good result, a creator layer for people who want to express something specific, and advanced controls for users who want to grow into lighting.
>
> If that works, EMU could bring in people who do not currently think of themselves as lighting users — musicians, dance creators and small creative teams. Some of them could later grow into ENTTEC hardware and, where relevant, ELM.
>
> I would validate that cheaply first through creator content and user experiments, then deepen it through Artist Lab and a music-to-light visual grammar developed with musicians. AI would come later only if we prove that intent-to-light translation is a repeated problem.
>
> What I’d really like to understand is whether this Product Specialist role has the user access, experimentation space, Engineering capacity and roadmap participation to run that kind of loop.

Then stop.

---

# 15. Do not lead with these

Unless the interviewer engages deeply, avoid starting with:

- Sia / Muni Long / LANY guest ideas
- long artist contracts
- full AI architecture
- deep CapCut integration
- shared-engine technical design
- “ENTTEC should do this” certainty

---

# 16. You are also interviewing ENTTEC

Validate:

- direct user access
- independent problem space
- experiment authority
- Engineering capacity
- roadmap participation
- ownership vs support
- 12-month domain
- discovery-to-outcome continuity

Enthusiasm for the ideas is not enough if those conditions are weak.
