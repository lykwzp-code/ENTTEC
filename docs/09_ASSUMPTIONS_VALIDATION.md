# 09 — Assumptions & Interview Validation

## Purpose

Keep three things separate:

1. **published ENTTEC facts**
2. **product / growth hypotheses**
3. **unknowns that require interview or internal validation**

---

## 1. Public facts used as grounding

The repository currently uses public ENTTEC information indicating that:

### EMU
- is creator / musician-oriented
- supports audio-reactive and music-linked workflows
- can serve smaller creator setups than ELM

### ELM
- is a professional pixel-mapping / spatial-production platform
- supports larger-scale professional workflows
- includes creative effects that have originated from EMU

### S-PLAY / hardware ecosystem
- supports deployment, automation and physical execution use cases

These facts justify the questions.

They do not prove the proposed strategy.

---

## 2. Core hypotheses to validate

### Product experience
- EMU can support progressive complexity: Quick → Creator → Advanced.
- templates can expand the beginner market without destroying professional depth.
- outcome-first onboarding can reduce the lighting-learning barrier.

### Market
- individual creators and small teams are a meaningful incremental market.
- dance / performance creators are a useful beachhead.
- creator content can generate qualified product demand.

### Portfolio
- EMU can become an ecosystem entry point.
- users can progress from EMU into ENTTEC hardware and, where appropriate, ELM.
- selected creative primitives can graduate across products.

### Research / AI
- musicians can help define a reusable Music-to-Light Visual Grammar.
- AI may later help translate intent into editable visual behaviour.

### Role
- Product Specialist can own part of discovery → experiment → decision → outcome.

---

## 3. Role questions

### Why did the role broaden?
> Since the role is now broader than EMU, what product problems or opportunities made the team decide to broaden it?

### Direct user access
> Would this role have direct access to users such as musicians, designers, integrators or other creators for product discovery?

### Independent problem space
> Is there a specific user journey, workflow or product problem that this role would be expected to own and improve over time?

### Experiment authority
> If we wanted to test a small workflow or prototype with users before committing major Engineering resources, could this role lead that experiment?

### Engineering capacity
> Which Engineering resources would support this role, and how are priorities decided when teams are busy?

### Roadmap participation
> At what stage would this role join roadmap or prioritisation discussions — before priorities are decided, or mainly after decisions are made?

### Broader scope
> Does “not limited to EMU” mainly mean supporting more products, or taking broader product responsibility?

### 12-month ownership
> What would you expect this person to own independently after the first year?

### Discovery-to-outcome
> If this role identifies and validates a user problem, would the same person stay involved through implementation, launch and post-launch learning?

---

## 4. Product / growth questions

### Beginner creator opportunity
> Does the team want EMU to remain mainly for users who already understand lighting, or is there interest in making it accessible to creators who do not yet think of themselves as lighting users?

### Progressive complexity
> How does the team think about balancing beginner simplicity with advanced control in EMU?

### Templates
> Are templates mainly seen as convenience features, or could they become an onboarding and education layer for new creators?

### Adjacent markets
> Has the team explored dance studios, performance creators or small creative teams as EMU users?

### Ecosystem progression
> Do you see EMU as a possible entry point into ENTTEC hardware and more professional workflows over time?

### Creator partnerships
> Have artist partnerships influenced product decisions, or mainly served as case studies / marketing?

### AI
> Has the team discussed using AI above the current audio-reactive layer — for example, helping creators translate artistic intent into an editable lighting starting point?

---

## 5. Strong signals

- direct user access
- clear problem ownership
- experiment authority
- real Engineering access
- roadmap participation
- named domain after 12 months
- end-to-end learning loop
- interest in expanding creator adoption
- clear product / Marketing / Engineering collaboration

---

## 6. Weak / ambiguous signals

Require follow-up:

- “You’ll touch many products.”
- “Everyone wears many hats.”
- “You’ll work globally.”
- “We’re entrepreneurial.”
- “There are lots of opportunities.”

These do not establish Product Authority.

---

## 7. Negative signals

- almost all work is reactive support
- no protected discovery time
- no direct user access
- no Engineering allocation mechanism
- roadmap decisions happen elsewhere
- broader scope only means more SKU support
- growth means becoming a support generalist
- no one can explain product success metrics

---

## 8. Interpretation framework

The role questions ultimately test:

**Access** — can I learn from real users?  
**Ownership** — can I focus on a problem?  
**Resources** — can I act?  
**Decision & accountability** — can I build judgement from outcomes?

The strategy questions test whether ENTTEC is interested in:

**category expansion + creator growth + portfolio progression**

Do not assume the answer is yes.
