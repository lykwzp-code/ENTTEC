# 10 — ENTTEC Interview Story (English)

## The interview is not about presenting a pile of ideas

The story is:

> **why music + light matters to me  
> → the user problem I see  
> → why EMU could be a useful experimentation entry point  
> → how I would validate the market cheaply  
> → how content can evolve into Artist Lab and product discovery  
> → when AI becomes relevant  
> → what I want the Product Specialist role to own**

That is much stronger than “I have an AI lighting idea.”

---

# 1. Start with the origin

> One of the experiences that first made me think seriously about music and light was Ryuichi Sakamoto’s *seeing sound, hearing time* exhibition in Beijing.
>
> What stayed with me was that the visual layer wasn’t simply synchronized to sound. Light, space and timing were part of the artistic expression itself.
>
> Later, experiences such as Hue + Spotify made the gap clearer to me: making lights react to music is not the same as helping a creator express what they want visually.
>
> That led me to a question: **how can a creator who is not a professional lighting designer turn an abstract feeling into an executable visual language?**

Pause.

That is the origin of the whole story.

---

# 2. Ask why the role changed before pitching

> Since the role is now broader than EMU, what product problems or opportunities made the team decide to broaden it?

Then:

> When you say “not limited to EMU”, does that mainly mean supporting more products, or taking broader product responsibility across software and hardware applications?

Understand the real role first.

---

# 3. Product hypothesis: EMU and ELM

If the role genuinely touches product strategy, software or user discovery:

> I noticed that ELM 2026 includes a large set of creative effects that originated in EMU.
>
> It made me wonder whether EMU could play a creator-facing experimentation role, while ELM remains the professional environment where selected mature ideas become scalable, deterministic, production-grade capabilities.
>
> I don’t know whether that matches how ENTTEC thinks internally, so I’d be interested in how you see the relationship between the two products.

Pause again.

---

# 4. Market validation: do not start with celebrity partnerships

> If I wanted to validate this direction, I wouldn’t start by spending heavily on major artists.
>
> I’d first test a simpler question: **are people interested in reinterpreting music visually through light, and does that interest translate into creator demand for EMU?**

A first content format could be:

**Reimagined in Light / Song to Light**

Each episode:

1. choose a rights-cleared song with a strong creative identity
2. define the feeling / visual language
3. reinterpret it through EMU
4. add dancers / creators where appropriate
5. publish the full process on YouTube
6. publish the finished performance on TikTok / Shorts
7. document product friction

Key line:

> **The content is not the end goal. It is a low-cost market-discovery and product-discovery mechanism.**

---

# 5. Why older music and dance creators can be useful

> I recently thought about older K-pop as an example. A track such as JYJ’s *Get Out* already has a strong visual memory — red, performance, choreography, dramatic space.
>
> The interesting exercise would not be recreating the original MV. It would be asking: if we stripped the work down to its emotional and spatial language, how would we reinterpret it today primarily through light?

That can attract:

- nostalgic fans
- visual-music audiences
- K-pop cover creators
- dance crews
- dance studios
- performance-content creators

The adjacent market hypothesis is:

> **Some dance creators do not just want to record choreography. They want to reinterpret music visually and make their content feel more complete.**

---

# 6. If that works, evolve into Artist Lab

Only after the content / creator market shows real signal:

> I’d evolve the format into an Artist Lab.

Principle:

> **Design partner first, endorsement second.**

Instead of asking:

> “What feature do you want?”

Ask:

- What should the audience feel here?
- Where should the light deliberately not follow the beat?
- What visual behaviour would ruin the song?
- Why should the second chorus feel different from the first?

The Product Specialist translates the answers into product hypotheses.

---

# 7. The return-episode mechanism

> If an artist asks for something EMU cannot do yet, I wouldn’t hide the limitation.
>
> We could build the best reduced version, document the gap, and if the capability is later developed, invite the artist back with another song.
>
> The audience sees the whole arc: creator intent, product limitation, experimentation, product change, and then the creator returning to validate it.

That creates:
- product learning
- trust
- recurring narrative
- long-term creator relationships

---

# 8. Community comments are hypotheses, not roadmap votes

> I’d like the product team and creators to interact openly with viewers, but I wouldn’t let likes directly control the roadmap.

If a comment says:

> “Can EMU understand lyrics?”

The job is to ask:

> Is the underlying need lyric semantics, narrative progression, song structure, or something else?

That is product discovery.

---

# 9. AI comes later

Only bring this up if Ebony discusses AI, automation or your AI background.

Core principle:

> **AI should be a creative translator, not an automatic taste-maker.**

Example:

> “I want the final chorus to feel much wider, but not more aggressive.”

AI can help turn that into an editable starting point:

- section
- spatial behaviour
- intensity
- movement
- transition
- sync / drift / restraint

But the creator remains the authority.

Key line:

> **The product shouldn’t decide what the music means. It should help the artist express what they mean.**

---

# 10. Bring the story back to the Product Specialist role

The message is not:

> “I have many creative ideas.”

It is:

> **I want to be the person who turns ambiguous user language into a testable product problem.**

Suggested wording:

> I see the Product Specialist role as a bridge from customer input to product decision participation — understanding the problem, testing the hypothesis, and helping decide whether it deserves product investment and where it belongs in the portfolio.

---

# 11. If asked what you would do in the first 3–6 months

## Days 0–30 — Learn
- use EMU seriously
- understand ELM
- shadow Product / Engineering / Support / Sales
- study real support patterns
- clarify decision rights and resource allocation

## Days 31–60 — Discover
- interview musicians / creators
- interview professional ELM users
- identify repeated problems
- test whether dance / performance creators are a real adjacent market

## Days 61–90 — Experiment
- run 2–3 rights-cleared Reimagined in Light pilots
- include at least one dance / performance creator
- test one small product hypothesis

## Months 4–6 — Prove
- measure qualified creator demand
- see whether creators keep using EMU
- decide whether Artist Lab is justified
- decide whether AI is actually needed
- evaluate any EMU → ELM relevance only after evidence

Important line:

> I wouldn’t join and immediately try to build my idea. I’d first validate whether the problem is real and repeated.

---

# 12. Two-minute compact version

> My interest in this space started from Ryuichi Sakamoto’s *seeing sound, hearing time* exhibition in Beijing. It made me realise that light shouldn’t just react to music — it can participate in artistic expression.
>
> That’s why EMU interests me. I think there may be a product opportunity between what a creator wants to express and the technical lighting controls they have to operate.
>
> I also noticed that ELM 2026 includes creative effects that originated in EMU, which made me wonder whether EMU could be a creator-facing place to experiment with new ideas, while selected mature capabilities eventually scale into professional workflows in ELM.
>
> I wouldn’t start by building a big AI feature or hiring major artists. I’d first test the market through a content format — reimagining rights-cleared music through light, working with dance creators, and seeing whether this creates real creator demand and product learning.
>
> If that works, I’d evolve it into an Artist Lab where musicians become design partners. AI could come later as a creative translator if we validate that creators really struggle to convert artistic intent into lighting language.
>
> What I’d like to understand is whether this Product Specialist role would actually have the user access, experimentation space and product decision participation to run that kind of loop.

Then stop.

---

# 13. Do not lead with these in the first interview

- Sia / Muni Long / LANY guest ideas
- multi-year artist contract details
- full AI architecture
- detailed shared-engine architecture
- strong claims about what ENTTEC “should” do
- assumptions about what the CEO already wants
- dumping the whole GitHub repository into the conversation

Use the repository as follow-up material if the interviewer wants to go deeper.

---

# 14. You are also interviewing ENTTEC

You need evidence on:

- direct user access
- owned problem space
- experimentation authority
- Engineering capacity
- roadmap participation
- whether “broader scope” means ownership or more support
- what you could own after 12 months
- whether you can stay involved from discovery to product outcome

If those are weak, enthusiasm for your ideas alone does not make this the right Product entry role.

## 5. 为什么旧歌和 dance creator 有价值

> 我最近想到旧 K-pop 就是一个例子。比如 JYJ 的 *Get Out* 本身已经有很强的视觉记忆：红色、performance、choreography、空间戏剧感。

## 6. 如果这条线成立，再做 Artist Lab

只有内容和 creator market 真的跑出 signal 后：

> 我才会进一步做 Artist Lab。

Product Specialist 把这些回答转成 product hypothesis。
